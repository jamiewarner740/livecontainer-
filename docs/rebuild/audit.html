Pre-rebuild audit

H&H Outlet (hisnhersllc.com)

A pallet-liquidation store with a capable back office and a storefront that doesn't sell yet. Shoppers can't buy without a Replit account, the catalog has nine items with no descriptions or categories, and the luxury-boutique styling doesn't fit $5 bargain pricing.

Audited 27 Nov 2026Last deploy 5 Aug 2026Pages checked 15 public routes, desktop + mobile
Verdict

Rebuild the storefront and keep the operations ideas. The admin side (barcode scanning, UPC lookup, Shippo labels, Square refunds, low-stock alerts) is well thought out and fits how a liquidation store works. The customer side has a few things that block sales outright. The first is the sign-in wall: it sends shoppers to Replit to make an account. The others are an empty homepage, no categories, and product photos that are screenshots of Amazon listings. None of the storefront code needs to be kept.

Fix these first

These five problems cost the most sales. Each one is a requirement for the rebuild.

  1. You can't buy without making a Replit account

    The cart, checkout and favorites all say “Sign in”, and the login page says “Secure authentication provided via Replit.” A shopper with a $5 item in mind has to sign up with a developer platform they've never heard of. There's no guest checkout. This alone could explain zero online sales.

  2. The homepage shows no products

    The featured-products and categories lists both come back empty, so the homepage is two hero banners, a marquee and a footer. The Categories page reads “No Divisions Found. We are currently reorganizing our archives.”

  3. Product photos are screenshots of Amazon listings

    Several images include Amazon's interface: “Hear the highlights 1:50”, “prime business”, color swatches and share icons. They're also cropped phone screenshots (1080×2410). This looks untrustworthy, and it uses another retailer's images and content.

  4. No store address, hours or map

    The site's own fallback text says “visit us in store”, but no page gives a location. Contact says only “Based in United States.” Liquidation stores depend on local foot traffic and “near me” searches, so this is the biggest missed opportunity.

  5. Copy promises a different business

    The trust badges say “Global Delivery”, “Authentic · Verified Quality”, “Premium · Highest Standards” and “Exclusive · Limited Drops.” Checkout asks you to “finalize your acquisition”, and categories are “The Archives.” For untested pallet stock at 70–86% off, this sets the wrong expectations. “Global” also contradicts the shipping policy.

What it looks like today

All findings

Grouped by area. Each finding ends with what the rebuild should do about it.

Conversion & checkout

Critical

Third-party login required for cart and checkout

Every customer account goes through Replit Auth (/api/login), with no email or password and no social login.

Guest checkout by default, with an optional account created after purchase.

High

“Quick add” and favorites also hit the login wall

Browsing works when signed out, but every action that commits to buying needs an account first.

Keep the cart in the browser (or on the server, tied to a session) with no login needed.

Medium

The free-shipping threshold is buried

“Free shipping on orders over $150” only appears in the shipping policy. Local pickup exists in checkout but isn't mentioned anywhere else.

Put the free-shipping threshold and free local pickup in the header bar and on product pages.

Medium

Footer shows payment badges that may not be offered

Apple Pay, Visa, Mastercard and Amex badges are in the footer, but checkout code only renders a card form. Admin has an Apple Pay domain setting, so check whether it's active.

Show only the payment methods checkout actually supports.

Low

“New Arrivals” link does nothing different

The footer links to ?sort=newest, but the product page reads sortBy. So it shows the same list as “Shop All.”

Make New Arrivals a real filter, for example items added in the last 7 days.

Catalog & content

Critical

Nine products, all added on one day

Product IDs 5–13 were all created on 4 Aug 2026, and the site hasn't been deployed since 5 Aug. All nine have no description, no category and none are featured. That contradicts the tagline “new inventory dropping constantly.”

Listing a product has to take under a minute from a phone. Keep the barcode-to-listing flow and make category a required field.

High

Every item is “SALE” with an unexplained compare-at price

For example, $5.00 shown against $34.99 as “86% OFF.” If those are MSRPs, the label should say so. Unsupported “was” prices are a consumer-protection risk.

Label it “Est. retail $34.99” with a short note on where retail prices come from. Save “Sale” for actual markdowns.

High

No condition information

Liquidation stock can be new, open-box, used or untested, and shoppers need to know which.

Add a condition field (New / Open box / Like new / As-is) shown as a badge, plus a clear as-is and returns note.

Medium

Messy product names

Examples: trailing spaces, “Kids: Age: 8-10 gloves”, inconsistent capitalization, and sizes inside titles (“size Large”).

Use a naming pattern (Brand + Item + key spec), and put size and color in their own fields.

Medium

No reviews or social proof anywhere

The review feature exists but has zero reviews. The site doesn't show Instagram or Facebook content or in-store photos.

Embed real in-store and pallet-unboxing photos, and link to Google reviews once a Business Profile exists.

Performance

High

The product grid downloads about 13 MB

Product photos are served at full phone resolution (1080×2410 up to 3072×4080, 0.5–1.8 MB each) for roughly 300 px cards. On mobile data, that's very slow.

Use an image CDN or build-time resizing to serve WebP/AVIF at 400 and 800 px with srcset, and lazy-load below the fold.

High

The logo is a 1.6 MB PNG and loads twice

logo.png is 1254×1254 but displays at about 40 px in the header and footer. The favicon is 199 KB, and the share image is a 765 KB 16-bit PNG.

Use an SVG logo, a 32/180 px favicon set, and an ~80 KB JPEG share image.

Medium

Blank page until about 400 KB of JavaScript runs

It's a client-rendered React app with about 400 KB of JS before code-split pages, and the first screen depends on several API calls.

Pre-render or server-render pages so the HTML arrives with content.

SEO & local search

High

Search engines get an empty page

The HTML is an empty <div id="root"> plus a <noscript> blurb. Product names, prices and descriptions only exist after JavaScript runs.

Server-render or pre-render pages, and add Product, Offer and LocalBusiness structured data.

High

Sitemap lists private pages and no products

sitemap.xml lists /cart, /profile and /orders, but no product URLs.

Generate the sitemap automatically from active products and categories, and keep account pages out of it.

Medium

Every page has the same meta description

Some titles repeat the brand twice (“Contact — H&H Outlet — H&H Outlet”), and cart, checkout and orders use the homepage title.

Give each page its own title and description. Product pages should include name, price and condition.

Medium

Missing pages return status 200

/nope-404 shows a “Lost in the Collection” page but returns HTTP 200, so search engines can index junk URLs.

Return a real 404 status. Redirect sold-out products to their category.

Medium

No analytics at all

No Google Analytics, Meta Pixel or any other tracking was loaded. There's no way to tell what's working.

Add privacy-friendly analytics (GA4, Plausible or PostHog) and a Meta Pixel if they run ads.

Accessibility

High

Pinch-zoom is blocked

The viewport tag sets maximum-scale=1, so low-vision shoppers can't zoom in on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4.

Remove maximum-scale.

Medium

Icon buttons have no labels

The products page has 11 buttons with no accessible name (favorite, share, menu), and the catalog search input has no label.

Add aria-labels and visible labels on form fields.

Medium

Low-contrast text

“In stock (50 available)” is dark grey on black. A lot of UI text is 10–11 px uppercase with wide letter-spacing.

Body text should be at least 4.5:1 contrast and 14 px.

Low

Some pages have no H1

Cart, favorites, checkout, profile and orders have no H1 when you're signed out.

Give every page exactly one H1.

Brand, design & trust

High

Three names in use

“His & Hers” (header), “H&H Outlet” (titles, hero) and “His & Hers LLC” (legal) are all used. The domain is hisnhersllc.com.

Pick one shopper-facing name (we'd suggest “H&H Outlet”) and use the LLC name only in legal text.

Medium

Contact email looks personal

The public address is abcottie13@hisnhersllc.com, which looks like a personal handle. There's no phone number.

Set up hello@ and orders@ aliases, and add a phone or text number.

Medium

Install prompt covers the main button on mobile

The “Add to Home Screen” popup appears on the first visit and sits over “Explore Collection.”

Drop the prompt, or show it only after a purchase or a third visit.

Low

Duplicate hero and filler marquee

The homepage has two near-identical taglines plus a repeating marquee of six phrases.

Replace them with “This week's drop”, shop by category, visit the store, and how pallet pricing works.

Low

Legal pages are generic

Terms set governing law as “the laws of the United States” with no state. Returns allow 14 days but don't say how as-is items are handled.

Name the state and add a plain-English returns section for liquidation stock. Have someone qualified review it.

Technical & security

Medium

Depends on Replit hosting

Hosting, login and file storage all run on Replit (his-and-hers-llc.replit.app). Images load through /api/storage/objects//objects/… (note the double slash) and redirect to signed URLs, so they can't be cached well.

Use standard hosting with a CDN for images, and export the product data and photos before switching.

Low

Every page logs a console error

/api/auth/user returns 401 for every signed-out visitor and shows up as an error. It's harmless but clutters error tracking.

Return 200 with null for signed-out visitors.

Low

Positives to keep

HTTPS with HSTS. Card entry uses Square's hosted fields, so card data never touches the site (simplest PCI scope). Shipping, privacy and terms pages exist. Order tracking links work for USPS, UPS, FedEx and DHL.

Keep Square and these policies.

Recommended rebuild

Option A · Shopify (fastest)

  • Good fit if the owner wants to run it alone with no developer.
  • Guest checkout, Shop Pay, Apple Pay, SEO, sitemaps and reviews come built in.
  • Shopify POS can replace Square for in-store sales so inventory stays in sync.
  • Trade-off: about $39+/mo plus apps, and the custom barcode-to-listing flow becomes an app or a manual step.

Option B · Custom rebuild (keeps the admin)

  • Good fit if the scan-to-list workflow is the main reason they built it.
  • Server-rendered storefront (Next.js or Astro), Postgres, an image CDN and email-link login.
  • Keep Square, Shippo and the admin features above, and connect to Square POS inventory.
  • Trade-off: someone has to maintain it.
Must-have
  • Guest checkout, a persistent cart with no login, and one clear brand name
  • Store address, hours, map and phone on every page, plus a Google Business Profile
  • Homepage that leads with this week's arrivals and shop by category
  • Real product photos, a condition badge, “Est. retail” price labels and short descriptions
  • Resized images, an SVG logo and server-rendered pages
Next
  • Product and LocalBusiness structured data, a product sitemap and correct 404s
  • Analytics plus Meta Pixel, and newsletter sign-up connected to an email tool
  • “Drop day” announcements tied to Instagram and Facebook posts
  • Phone-friendly listing flow: scan, take a photo, set price and condition, publish
Later
  • Hold for in-store pickup, and pallet or bulk sales for resellers
  • Review requests after pickup or delivery

How this was checked

Every public page was loaded in a headless Chrome browser at desktop (1440 px) and iPhone 13 sizes. I recorded the network requests, console errors, page text, image sizes and basic accessibility checks, and read the site's public JavaScript to list features and integrations. Nothing was purchased. Account and admin pages were seen only in their signed-out state, and admin access controls weren't tested. Before launch, confirm product counts, order volume and which payment methods are enabled with the owner in the admin dashboard.
